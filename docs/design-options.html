Design directions — round 2

Five directions, all showing the same house on the same evening. Unlike the first round, the hero of every layout is a visualisation, not a checklist — trends, rings, distributions, load over time. Tasks are still there, because the house genuinely tracks them, but they sit where they belong: one signal among many.

Say a number, or mix (“4’s density with 2’s warmth” is a perfectly good answer). Whatever you pick becomes static/nora_home/css/nora-home.css and the ECharts theme derives from the same variables, so charts and chrome can never drift apart.

What changed from round 1. The old mockups led with a “Today” list and put a bar chart in the corner — which is why they read as a todo app. These lead with a full-width trend, a household ring set, a distribution, and a year heatmap. Every direction below carries the same eight widgets so the comparison is honest.
Option 1

Observatory

a quiet monitoring console

Cool cyan and indigo on near-black. Built for reading many numbers at once without fatigue — the hero is a full-width trend with a gradient fill, and everything else orbits it. This is what a house that also runs a robot probably wants.

If you want a recommendation

Option 1 (Observatory) as the base, with Option 2's rings on the home screen. Observatory is the only one that stays readable as the app count grows past a handful, needs no separate light variant for a screen that is on all day, and handles both a dense maintenance log and a wall panel without compromise. Borrowing Vitals' ring set for the household-consistency widget gives the family-facing screen the warmth Observatory lacks on its own.

Option 4 (Flight Deck) is the better answer if the robot telemetry ends up being the thing you look at most — it is genuinely the most legible across a room, and the density is real, not styling. The cost is that it will never feel welcoming to anyone but you.

Option 5 (Terrazzo) is the one the rest of the family will like most. Its ceiling is real though: colour is doing the encoding, and once eight or ten apps each want a hue, the system stops meaning anything.

Say a number and I will build it out properly — full type scale, spacing, components, the matching ECharts theme, and verification at 375px, on the 1080p wall, and on the 1280×800 kiosk.
