Round 3, corrected — Almanac

The first pass showed a passive "ambient wall" — that's not this house. The 24" always shows the real, fully interactive /home/ app (or whatever app someone picked on the kiosk); the 10.1" is strictly a button remote and never mirrors the app itself. This pass keeps the living atmosphere — season, time of day, weather — but puts it behind the actual product, shown as it's actually built: the two screens together, side by side, the same moment.

What's shown below. The 24" wall across four different seasons, times of day, and weather conditions — full nav, full widget grid, a different app selected in two of the four to prove this isn't a skin on one screen. The 10.1" kiosk paired next to it three times, at the same moment as the wall, plus its per-app control screen. Then the phone, full functionality, same living background. Laptop is skipped — same layout as the 24" wall, just narrower.
The engine

Three independent axes composite behind every screen: season sets the horizon and foliage, time of day sets the sky and the sun or moon's position, weather adds rain, snow, or sun on top. All three come from data the house already has or can get cheaply — the system clock, and once wired up, an outdoor weather integration (Story 25).

Open questions, before this goes further

  • Real weather source. Every "outside" number here is invented for the mockup, but tying the theme to the actual weather is a real, reachable build, not a stretch goal: a free source like Open-Meteo (no API key) or Home Assistant's own weather entity (Story 25) gets polled every 10–15 minutes by the existing integration framework, its condition code buckets into clear / cloudy / rain / snow, and that one value gets pushed to both screens over the same display websocket the kiosk already uses to drive the wall — so the sky updates live, not just on a page reload. Season and time of day need no integration at all; they already come from the system clock. Worth sequencing Story 25 earlier than planned if this direction is the one.
  • Kiosk legibility under the atmosphere. The kiosk's whole job is fast, unambiguous taps — worth checking the living background never competes with a tile at arm's length, especially at night when the sky is darkest and tiles need to still read instantly.
  • Two screens, one moment. The wall and kiosk need to agree on season/time/weather in real time so they don't drift out of sync — a shared piece of state both templates read, not two independent clocks.
  • Performance on the Pi. Backdrop blur on every pane, continuous small animations (rain, snow, stars), on two Chromium instances at once on a Pi 5 — untested. May need to trade blur for a flat tint, or cap concurrent particles.
  • How far the illustration goes. This mockup hand- places concrete states; a real build needs the axes to interpolate continuously — sunrise eases rather than jumping from "dawn" to "noon."
