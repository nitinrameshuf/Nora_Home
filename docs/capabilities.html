Nora Home

What the house can do

Nora Home is the operating system for our house. It runs on a Raspberry Pi in the hall, it is on all the time, and it is built so that any of us can add an app to it without rebuilding the plumbing underneath.

Living document · reflects the code as of 31 July 2026 · version 0.1.0 · live works today · partial built but unproven · planned not yet written

Where things stand. The platform — the part every app plugs into — is written and running. What does not exist yet is the apps themselves: workout, diet, maintenance, robot monitoring. That is the point of building the skeleton first, and it is what comes next.

Running the house live

The tracker is the spine. Anything with a deadline — a chore, a habit, a filter change, a medication — is registered once and then the house owns it.

  • Recurring anything live

    Daily, weekdays, weekly, monthly, quarterly, yearly, every N days, or a cron expression. Due instances are written two weeks ahead, so "what did I miss last March" stays answerable.

  • Escalation when things slip live

    A ladder, not a nag. It nudges the person, warns them, then tells whoever is on their escalation list, then tells the whole house. Each rung has its own delay, and acknowledging it stops the climb. Editable without touching code.

  • Streaks and reliability live

    Completions and misses are both recorded, so consistency is measurable rather than remembered.

  • Evidence live

    A completion can carry a note, a number, or a photo when "I did it" isn't enough.

Telling people live

  • Slack live

    Direct messages to individuals via a bot token, or a single channel via an incoming webhook. Escalations always also land in the house alert channel, so nothing quietly dies in a DM.

  • The wall display live

    Alerts take over the 24" screen for a while, then hand it back. A critical one stays until someone acknowledges it.

  • In the app live

    An inbox, a live badge, and Nora glancing toward it when something arrives.

  • Quiet hours live

    Per person. Ordinary notifications wait; genuine alerts do not.

  • Delivery receipts live

    Every attempt on every channel is recorded, with retries. "Did anyone actually see this?" has an answer.

  • Phone push planned

    Home-screen install and web push. Slack covers urgent delivery until then.

The two screens live

The Pi drives both HDMI outputs. The small screen is the remote control for the big one.

24" wall display

Always on. Rotates through panels — the household's day, streaks, whatever apps contribute. Dims at night rather than switching off, and shifts by a few pixels every few minutes so it never burns in.

10.1" kiosk

Touch. Tap a panel and the wall holds it while you look, then resumes on its own. Also: next, back, dim, wake, reload.

  • Survives everything live

    If the network drops, the wall keeps rotating on a local timer. If an app breaks, its panel is skipped rather than blanking the screen. If a screen goes dark for ten minutes, the house says so.

  • Any screen can join live

    A spare tablet opening the wall URL registers itself and joins the rotation.

The home screen live

  • Yours, not everyone's live

    Each person arranges their own grid, picking visualizations from any app in the house. Drag to move, drag to resize, and it is remembered.

  • Charts that match live

    Apps supply data, not styling. The house supplies colour, type, and dark/light, so a chart written by one of us looks like a chart written by another.

  • Works offline live

    The charting libraries live in the repo. Nothing is fetched from the internet to draw a screen.

Measuring things live

  • One place for every number live

    Weight, sleep, room temperature, the robot's battery, a portfolio value, litres of water. All the same shape: a named number, at a time, optionally about a person.

  • Thresholds that speak up live

    Give a series a warning and an alert bound and crossing it notifies the right person by itself.

  • Kept without bloating live

    Hourly summaries mean a year-long chart doesn't read a million rows on a Pi.

Nora, and AI partial

  • Nora herself live

    The little bot in the corner. She wanders when idle, zips to whatever you just touched, and speaks when the house has something to say. She stays off the wall display — nobody is standing there to talk to.

  • Claude, wired to the house's own data partial

    Three model tiers so an app asks for a capability rather than naming a model. Needs an API key to switch on.

  • A monthly budget live

    Every call's cost is recorded. Past the cap the house declines rather than spending. A runaway loop costs one refusal, not a credit card.

  • Agents can read the house live

    An MCP server exposes what the house knows — who lives here, what is open, what is overdue, how reliable someone is, any measurement — to Claude Code, Claude Desktop, or the robot. Apps add their own tools in about ten lines.

Connecting to the outside partial

  • Integration framework live

    Scheduling, retries with backoff, failure alerting, and credential handling are all provided. An integration only has to say how to fetch.

  • Home Assistant, markets, weather, calendars planned

    The framework is ready; no concrete integrations are written yet.

  • Credentials stay out of the database live

    Tokens are read from the environment, so a database copied for debugging carries none of them.

Not losing anything live

  • Nightly backups live

    Relational data, documents, uploaded files, and a portable export — every night at half three, with old ones pruned automatically. If a backup fails, the house says so; a backup nobody notices breaking is not a backup.

  • Restore, and move live

    Two paths: an exact restore onto the same setup, and a portable one that moves the whole house onto different database software or a different machine.

  • Nothing vanishes by accident live

    Records that would hurt to lose are hidden rather than destroyed, and can be brought back.

Adding an app live

This is what the whole thing is for. An app declares itself, and the platform gives it a URL, a place in the menu, a slot on the home screen and the wall, plus scheduling, reminders, escalation, notifications, AI, charts, and storage. 

make app SRC=https://github.com/you/nora-workout

That clones it, registers it, migrates the database, and restarts. It is then live at /workout/. The instructions an app author (or their AI agent) needs are in DEVELOPMENT.md.

Keeping it running live

  • One command live

    make deploy pulls, rebuilds, migrates, and restarts. There is no separate migration step to forget.

  • Starts with the Pi live

    Both screens come up on their own after a power cut, and wait for the app to answer before opening a window.

  • Health checks live

    Every dependency is probed. Losing documents is "degraded"; losing the main database is "down". The house tells the family which.

  • Traceable live

    Every log line carries a request id, who was acting, and which screen they were on, so "why did the wall do that at 6am" is answerable from the logs.
