{% load static django_vite nh %} {% comment %} The 10.1" touchscreen on the Pi's second HDMI output, rebuilt in Story 50 as a piece of hardware rather than a web page: an app scroller, a bank of illuminated keys, two faders, a scroll wheel, a power key, and a dot-matrix readout. It never shows the app itself — it tells the 24" wall what to show. Design language is the mockup's (@layer kiosk), taken from Teenage Engineering: monospace only, a tight palette, few well-placed controls, limitation as the aesthetic. **Every key is a path.** navigate / refresh / banner is the entire vocabulary wall-live.js implements, so a key can only ever send the wall somewhere. An app declares nora_kiosk_controls and its keys appear with no platform change; an app that declares none gets a single "Open" key rather than an empty bank. **Zoom and the bend wheel went live in Story 51.** Zoom nudges the wall's stored zoom and reloads it; the wheel sends a scroll *rate* the wall integrates. **One control is still deliberately dead.** Wall-only power has no working mechanism on this hardware — Story 51 tested six, including as root, and every one is disproved (see docs/Main_App/progress.md). It carries `disabled` and `.is-dead` and reads as unlit rather than as a control that quietly does nothing. See base.html's note on zoom. The kiosk's own ceiling is lower than the wall's (nora_home/ui/zoom.py) because at 1024 physical, a large zoom puts the layout viewport under the 860px breakpoint while media queries still report 1024. {% endcomment %} {{ house_name }} — kiosk {% csrf_token %}
