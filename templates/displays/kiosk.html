{% load static django_vite nh %} {% comment %} The 10.1" touchscreen on the Pi's second HDMI output, rebuilt in Story 50 as a piece of hardware rather than a web page: an app scroller, a bank of illuminated keys, two faders, a scroll wheel, a power key, and a dot-matrix readout. It never shows the app itself — it tells the 24" wall what to show. Design language is the mockup's (@layer kiosk), taken from Teenage Engineering: monospace only, a tight palette, few well-placed controls, limitation as the aesthetic. **Every key is a path.** navigate / refresh / banner is the entire vocabulary wall-live.js implements, so a key can only ever send the wall somewhere. An app declares nora_kiosk_controls and its keys appear with no platform change; an app that declares none gets a single "Open" key rather than an empty bank. **Some of this desk is deliberately dead.** The two faders, the scroll wheel and the wall power key are drawn because the desk's composition is the design — but the capabilities behind them do not exist yet and belong to Story 51 (zoom from the panel, a scroll message wall-live.js does not implement, a host mixer call for volume, and wall-only power, which CLAUDE.md records as unsolved and which Story 51's own warn says must be proven on hardware before it ships). They carry `disabled` and `.is-dead`, so they read as unlit rather than as controls that quietly do nothing. See base.html's note on zoom. The kiosk's own ceiling is lower than the wall's (nora_home/ui/zoom.py) because at 1024 physical, a large zoom puts the layout viewport under the 860px breakpoint while media queries still report 1024. {% endcomment %} {{ house_name }} — kiosk {% csrf_token %}
